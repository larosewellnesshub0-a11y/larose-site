Scientific update: a normal ultrasound does not always end an abdominal-pain assessment

Published: 9 August 2026 2 min read Dr. Mohab Ashraf Fouad

Medically reviewed by: Dr. Shimaa Fouad

On this page

  1. A normal result has limits
  2. Read the impression with the clinician

A normal result has limits

RadiologyInfo, reviewed by US radiology societies, explains that abdominal ultrasound can show organs such as the liver, gallbladder, kidneys and spleen well, while sound waves are disrupted by air and gas. Air-filled bowel or an organ hidden behind it may therefore be incompletely seen.

A normal report means that no problem was seen in the structures that could be assessed with that method. It does not cancel every possible cause of abdominal pain. Sometimes the question is answered; sometimes history, examination or blood results point to a different test.

Read the impression with the clinician

Bring the complete report and images if available, then ask what was seen well, whether any area was obscured and whether the result explains the symptoms. Do not automatically repeat the same examination when the question has changed, and do not treat a different follow-up test as a contradiction; each method has strengths and limits.

Severe worsening pain, a rigid abdomen, fainting, persistent vomiting, bleeding, marked fever or jaundice needs urgent assessment even if an earlier ultrasound was normal. The current condition matters more than an old reassuring report.

Medical sources

  1. RadiologyInfo (ACR/RSNA)، Abdominal Ultrasound

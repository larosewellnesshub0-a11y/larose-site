## Original coverage brief

Answer the immediate question first: a low ferritin can fit iron deficiency before the CBC or haemoglobin meets criteria for anaemia, but one number cannot diagnose the cause or dictate a dose. Explain what ferritin, haemoglobin, MCV/MCH, transferrin saturation, CRP/inflammation and the person's history each contribute. Include Egyptian-patient wording around heavy periods, pregnancy/postpartum status, diet, blood donation, antacid/acid-suppression use, coeliac disease or other malabsorption, and gastrointestinal blood loss. Make clear when a clinician may consider gastrointestinal investigation and when symptoms such as black stools, blood, fainting, breathlessness at rest, chest pain, rapidly worsening weakness, unexplained weight loss, or persistent vomiting need prompt assessment.

Do not provide a universal oral-iron or IV-iron dose. Use authoritative English clinical sources for all claims, while keeping the patient-facing search language Arabic and Egyptian Arabic. Link naturally to existing pages covering a balanced iron-containing diet, fatigue assessment, heavy menstrual bleeding/pregnancy where suitable, gastrointestinal symptom assessment, and the internal-medicine hub.

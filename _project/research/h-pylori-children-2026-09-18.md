## Original coverage brief

Lead with the parent-safe answer: H. pylori can matter in selected pediatric situations, but a recurrent stomachache, poor appetite or bloating alone does not prove it or automatically make a stool test, breath test, endoscopy or antibiotics right. Explain the difference between a symptom story, a test for active infection, an endoscopy chosen for a different clinical question, and confirmation after treatment. Include the need to tell the team about antibiotics and acid-suppressing medicines before testing, without giving a self-directed stop/start regimen.

Cover red flags: blood or black stool, bloody/green or repeated vomiting, dehydration or inability to drink, severe/localising pain, fever with deterioration, fainting, marked lethargy, weight loss or growth concerns. Link naturally to the sibling testing-decision page, pediatric abdominal-pain/growth and iron-deficiency pages, the pediatric hub, and the H. pylori after-treatment page. Avoid universal antibiotic regimens, universal test timing, or an implication that family members should self-test.

## Canonical rewrite outline and citation allocation

1. **Answer first:** a recurring tummy ache does not prove H. pylori; explain why testing is a clinical question. Cite 1–3.
2. **What parents may notice:** upper-abdominal symptoms can overlap with reflux, constipation, infection, food intolerance and functional pain. Cite 4, 6, 7.
3. **When a clinician may consider testing:** ulcer disease, endoscopic findings, selected refractory iron-deficiency context; distinguish this from routine testing for functional pain. Cite 1–3, 5.
4. **Stool, breath and endoscopy:** explain that each test has a purpose and preparation/medicine history matters; do not give a self-directed medicine-stop schedule. Cite 1, 2, 8.
5. **Treatment and confirmation:** no shared antibiotic course or family self-treatment; confirmation after a clinician-selected course is a separate question. Cite 1–3, 9, 10.
6. **Growth, hydration and urgent signs:** poor growth, blood/black stool, green/bloody or repeated vomiting, severe/localising pain, inability to drink or marked lethargy require timely assessment. Cite 5, 7.
7. **Visit checklist and next step:** symptoms, timing, food/stool/vomiting record, medicine list, earlier results, growth concerns; link naturally to the child testing-decision page and pediatric hub. Cite 1, 2, 7.

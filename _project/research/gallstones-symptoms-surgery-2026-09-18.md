## Original coverage brief

Open by separating ultrasound finding, typical biliary pain, acute complication signs, and an operation decision. Explain that a stone found incidentally is not the same as symptomatic disease; that post-meal discomfort, reflux, bowel symptoms and upper-abdominal pain can overlap; and that a clinician may use history, examination, blood tests, ultrasound and occasionally other imaging to answer different questions. Address the user’s likely worries: "هل لازم أشيل المرارة؟", "الألم بعد الأكل ده مرارة؟", and what an ultrasound result can or cannot confirm.

Link naturally to the abdominal-ultrasound result guide, gallbladder pain in pregnancy, the operation-day/recovery pages, persistent bloating assessment, and the gastroenterology hub. Use authoritative guidelines and public-health/academic sources for medical claims; never recommend a universal surgery date, diet, antibiotic, painkiller or home remedy. Include urgent signs such as persistent severe pain, fever or chills, jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, repeated vomiting, fainting, vomiting blood, black stool or rapid deterioration.
